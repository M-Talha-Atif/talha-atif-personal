# Job Search Strategy

Last updated: July 2026

---

## Core Strategy

Backend-anchored full stack engineer targeting remote roles at small-to-mid product companies in high-tech countries. Not chasing volume for its own sake — chasing signal quality first, then volume once signal is fixed.

The strategy has three simultaneous tracks running at all times:

1. **Fix the signal.** LinkedIn identity, GitHub presentation, portfolio, and a documented career history (career.md, achievements.md, metrics.md, projects.md, technical-decisions.md) all need to say the same thing consistently: backend-anchored engineer who owns systems end to end.
2. **Apply with precision, not spray.** Every application uses a tailored resume pulling from the same underlying story bank, not a generic resume sent everywhere.
3. **Build proof in public.** Deploy Moodly properly, keep contributing to open source, post real work stories, so that by the time someone looks you up, the evidence is already there.

---

## Weekly Cadence

- **Applications:** 20-30 per day, tailored per posting using the resume templates and story bank already built. Quality of tailoring matters more than raw count.
- **Content:** 1 LinkedIn post per week from a real work story (pull from career.md Experience Log).
- **Open source:** 1 PR per weekend maximum. Not the primary strategy, but keeps visibility compounding.
- **Mock interviews / prep:** 2 per week — one technical, one behavioral/system design — using the story bank in career.md and technical-decisions.md.
- **Review cycle:** Every Sunday, review what converted (replies, screens, interviews) and adjust which resume variant, which platforms, and which outreach angle is working.

---

## What "Working" Looks Like

Track these signals weekly, not just offers:

- Reply rate on cold outreach / DMs
- Recruiter screen rate from applications
- Technical interview rate from screens
- Where the drop-off happens most (resume screen vs recruiter call vs technical round)

If reply rate is low: the problem is positioning (resume, LinkedIn headline, outreach message), not effort.
If recruiter screens don't convert to technical rounds: the problem is the pitch/summary being told live.
If technical rounds don't convert to offers: the problem is interview delivery, not the underlying stories (since career.md confirms the stories are real and strong).

---

## Non-Negotiables

- Never apply to companies based in India or Turkey, regardless of remote status or role quality.
- Never take a role with zero real engineering ownership (pure ticket execution) unless it is a clear, temporary bridge with an explicit growth path stated upfront.
- Never misrepresent scale, metrics, or ownership in a resume — everything traces back to career.md and metrics.md, which only contain measured, defensible numbers.
- Never send a generic, non-personalized cold message — every outreach references something specific about the company's actual problem.

---

## Review and Iteration

This strategy is not static. Revisit monthly:

- Is the target role priority (Backend > Full Stack > Founding Engineer) still right, or should it shift based on what's actually converting?
- Is the geographic constraint (no India, no Turkey) limiting volume too much, and does it need reconsideration?
- Are the resume variants still accurately representing current skills, or has new work (open source, new job, new project) made them stale?
