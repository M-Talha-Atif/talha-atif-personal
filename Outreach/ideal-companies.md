# Ideal Companies

Last updated: July 2026

---

## What "Ideal" Means Here

A company where the engineering problems are genuinely hard, ownership is real (not ticket execution), the team is small enough that individual contribution is visible, and the company is stable enough that the role is not immediately at risk.

---

## Company Type Preferences

**Want:**
- Startups, especially 20-30 people with room to grow
- Product companies (not agencies, not pure outsourcing shops)
- Developer tools and infrastructure companies
- SaaS companies with real technical complexity
- Fintech (with genuine regulatory/compliance depth, not just a wrapper around a bank API)
- AI companies building real infrastructure, not thin wrappers around a single API call
- B2B software with technical buyers
- Companies backed by credible investors (YC, top-tier VCs) as a signal of technical bar, not as a prestige requirement

**Avoid:**
- Low engineering maturity shops (no code review culture, no testing discipline, no documentation)
- Pure outsourcing / staff-augmentation agencies, unless compensation is exceptional and the actual client work is technically interesting
- Roles with no meaningful engineering ownership — pure ticket-pushing with no architectural input
- Companies with unclear or shifting product direction where engineering effort gets thrown away frequently

---

## Geographic Constraint (Firm)

**Only target:** Companies based in the US, UK, Canada, EU, and other high-tech hub countries (e.g. Singapore, UAE for tech-forward companies).

**Explicitly excluded:** India-based companies, Turkey-based companies. This applies regardless of remote status, compensation, or role quality. This is a firm constraint, not a soft preference.

## Company Research Checklist (Before Applying)

For any new company under consideration, confirm:

1. Not based in India or Turkey
2. Team size and structure — is there room for real ownership, or is this a large team with narrow scope per engineer?
3. What technical problem are they actually solving — is it genuinely hard, or is it a thin CRUD wrapper?
4. Funding/stability signal — funded, profitable, or clear runway indication
5. Engineering culture signals — do they mention code review, testing, documentation, or ownership explicitly in their posting?
