# Outreach Rules

Last updated: July 2026

---

## Communication Style (Non-Negotiable)

- Technically precise communication over vague reassurance
- Direct language over hedged, padded language
- Evidence over generic claims — a claim without a number or a specific project behind it does not go in outreach
- Thoughtful personalization — every message references something specific about the company or role
- No fake enthusiasm, no forced excitement
- No generic openers like "I hope this email finds you well"
- No em dashes in any written communication
- No begging tone — every message should read as "here is why this is a fit," not "please consider me"

---

## Structure of a Strong Cold Message / DM

Every outreach message follows this shape, in this order:

1. **One line of real context** — what you actually did, stated plainly, not "I have experience in X"
2. **One line connecting to why this specific company/role is interesting** — must reference something specific from their posting or mission, not generic enthusiasm
3. **One or two lines mapping your real experience to their stated requirements** — pull directly from career.md stories, use their language back at them where it's accurate
4. **Optional: one honest line about a gap**, framed as the transferable skill rather than an apology
5. **A clear, low-friction next step** — portfolio link, offer to share CV, no "let me know if you'd like to chat" filler

Never pad the message with filler sentences. If a sentence doesn't do one of the five jobs above, cut it.

---

## Rules for Specific Channels

**Email applications:**
- Subject line matches exactly what the company's posting asks for (e.g. "Remote Job Application — Java Developer")
- State the position title explicitly
- Include notice period / availability if requested
- Attach CV, reference 2-3 relevant project links max
- Never attach company/client project files or reference NCA-protected work without noting the restriction explicitly

**LinkedIn DMs:**
- Reference the specific post or role, not a generic "I saw you're hiring"
- Keep to 4-6 sentences maximum
- Do not mention the tech stack verbatim if the person asked to keep it people-focused rather than tool-focused — lead with problem and ownership, not a list of technologies

**Cold outreach to founders/co-founders:**
- Assume they are reading fast — front-load the most relevant fact
- Reference their company's actual mission or technical problem, proving you read the posting, not just the title
- One honest sentence acknowledging any experience gap (e.g. years of experience, specific language like Kotlin) framed as "what I have instead"

---

## What Never Goes in Outreach

- Inflated metrics not backed by metrics.md
- Claims of experience with a technology not actually used (state "familiar" or "transferable" instead, matching resume conventions)
- Generic enthusiasm phrases: "passionate about," "excited to leverage," "synergy," "rockstar," "ninja"
- Apologetic framing for lack of years of experience — state what you have instead of what you lack
- Company project URLs or internal repo links protected by NCA — note the restriction plainly instead

---

## Follow-Up Rules

- If no response after initial outreach, one follow-up after 5-7 business days is acceptable
- Follow-up must add new information (a shipped update, a relevant project, a specific point about the role) — never just "following up on my previous message" with nothing new
- After one follow-up with no response, move on. Do not send a third message.

---

## Tracking

Every outreach sent should be logged with: date, company, role, channel, resume variant used, and outcome (no response / screen / rejection / interview). This feeds back into strategy.md's weekly review cycle to identify what messaging and positioning is actually converting.
