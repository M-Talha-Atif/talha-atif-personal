Dear Dawnguard Hiring Team,

I am writing to apply for the Medior Software Engineer, Full Stack role. I have also prepared
a page matched directly against this posting, available here,
\href{https://talha-atif.vercel.app/r/dawnguard}{talha-atif.vercel.app/r/dawnguard}. What drew me in
specifically is the idea of shift-left security done properly: validating design and generating
production-ready infrastructure as code before a single line of application code exists, rather
than bolting security on after the fact. That is a genuinely different way to think about the
problem, and it is close to how I already think about the systems I build.

My most recent role was on a compliance-heavy Identity Governance and Access Management
platform, where I owned the Access Portal end to end, the service layer handling request
lifecycle state, the Next.js and TypeScript operational UI, and the audit trail underneath all
of it. Security and auditability were not an afterthought there either, every access decision
had to be explainable after the fact. I built a Kafka-based event-driven approval pipeline with
dead-letter queues for reliable failure recovery, and refactored the entire audit log schema to
remove personal data while preserving forensic traceability, the kind of compliance-versus-
usability trade-off I imagine comes up constantly at Dawnguard.

On the stack side, I work in TypeScript daily across NestJS and Express services and Next.js
frontends, with real experience in event-driven design (Kafka, RabbitMQ), NoSQL data (MongoDB,
Redis), and Docker-based CI/CD pipelines. Claude and GitHub Copilot are part of my everyday
workflow already, not an occasional tool, which lines up directly with how your team wants to
use AI agents to write secure, stable, and maintainable TypeScript.

I have not worked with Angular or Pulumi specifically, and I want to say that plainly rather
than imply otherwise. My frontend production experience is in React and Next.js, and my
infrastructure-as-code exposure has been lighter-touch than owning a Pulumi codebase directly.
What I can say honestly is that I pick up new frameworks quickly when the underlying systems
problem, in this case secure, typed, event-driven full stack development, is one I already
understand and care about.

I would welcome the chance to talk through where my background fits and where it would need
to grow. Thank you for considering my application.
