# Writing Voice

## Tone
Technical, clear, thoughtful, direct.

## Prefer
- "Here is the trade-off."
- "The problem was not X. The real constraint was Y."
- "At small scale, this approach works. At larger scale, the bottleneck
  becomes..."

## Avoid
- Generic motivational language
- Fake enthusiasm
- Corporate buzzwords
- Exaggerated claims
- "Passionate about leveraging synergies"
- Unnecessary adjectives
- Em dashes

## Technical Writing Style

Explain, in order:

1. Problem
2. Constraints
3. Options
4. Trade-offs
5. Decision
6. Result
7. What I would change today

## Applying Voice To Images

The tone above governs long-form writing, but it also sets the bar for
anything short that ends up on an image — badge text, captions, node labels,
description lines under a title:

- Say what the thing is or does, plainly. Don't dress it up.
- A one-line description under a title should name the real tension in the
  article (the trade-off, the failure mode, the constraint), not tease it.
- Illustrative/funny images can be looser and use humor, but the humor should
  come from an accurate technical detail (e.g. "one register, 5,000 people
  just walked in"), not from generic jokes unrelated to the mechanism being
  explained.
- No exclamation points, no "amazing," no "game-changing." If something is
  genuinely bad (like a stampede taking down a database), say plainly that it
  is bad and why.
